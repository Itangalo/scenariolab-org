How it works

You describe a world in plain text: its actors, their goals, the starting situation and the events that might occur. A few quantitative variables keep track of the temperature of the world – resources, tensions, progress, or whatever the scenario demands. Language models play the actors, and also act as game master, deciding what happens next.

Why many runs

A single scenario may convince because it is specific. For the same reason, it cannot tell you how likely it is or what could have happened instead. Running many simulations from the same premises shows which outcomes keep recurring, which assumptions matter, and which decisions hold up across very different futures.

This is the idea behind Robust Decision Making, a method long used in climate and water policy. It has needed a formal mathematical model of the system, and fields like politics and governance have none. Language models can stand in for the strict mathematical models. The result is not a forecast, and the models bring biases of their own. Because runs are cheap, those weaknesses can be tested instead of taken on trust.
